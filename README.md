# Summer-Olympics-Dashboard

This dashboard explores 128 years of Summer Olympic competition using a cleaned dataset of 249,360 athlete-event entries spanning Athens 1896 through Paris 2024. The dataset's inclusion of all competitors (as opposed to just medalists) enables meaningful analysis across six charts, including women's participation trends, total athlete growth over time, sport-level gender balance, and athlete-level medal counts. All cleaning was performed in Python (pandas) across nine Jupyter cells, applying five documented decisions – dropping the non-IOC-recognized 1906 Games, removing duplicate entries concentrated in Art Competition records, dropping an unreliable player_id column, recoding dissolved-nation NOC codes (Soviet Union, East Germany, etc.) to modern map-compatible country names, and correcting known garbled athlete names introduced during the Numbers-to-Excel file conversion. The cleaned file (249,360 rows × 11 columns) was exported as olympics_clean.csv and connected to Tableau Desktop.

The Tableau workbook (Olympics_Dashboard.twbx) contains six charts and four KPI tiles. Charts 1 and 2 show geographic dominance by country and total athlete participation over time; Chart 3 shows the top most decorated athletes by medal type; Chart 4 tracks women's participation from 0% in 1896 to ~50% in Paris 2024; Chart 5 depicts women’s medal rate over time; and Chart 6 shows sport-level gender balance across the Top 10 sports by athlete count. While we had originally included seven charts, we decided to remove one (a chart depicting the top 15 nations by medal type) to minimize overlap with our map chart and redirect attention to our overarching narrative on rising participation among female athletes. Additionally, a global Year range slider allows all six charts to be filtered simultaneously, and the KPI tiles at the top of the dashboard comprise a high-level overview of key Olympic statistics.

The workbook was built sequentially across our group of three, each opening the prior version's .twbx from a shared Google Drive folder and adding their assigned charts before saving as the next version. The packaged workbook format bundles the CSV data inside, so no separate file management was required. Known limitations include: 1,733 rows tagged as 1906 dropped; 790 unrecognized Tableau location entries representing historical vessel and team names excluded from the map only; and a few athlete names left uncorrected pending manual verification against the original Kaggle source. For athletes listed in Chart 4, manual cleaning was performed using to ensure that misspellings were rectified and athletes with no known identity (e.g., John Jr.) were removed.

This project was completed with assistance from generative AI. We leveraged ChatGPT to clean / troubleshoot our Python code, validate our proposed structure / plan for each chart, and generate suggestions for chart design. All analytical decisions, chart design choices, and Tableau configurations were made and executed by the team. Finally, the AI tool explained the rationale for aesthetic design suggestions, navigated Mac-specific Tableau mechanics encountered during the build, and helped troubleshoot data discrepancies; it did not generate the workbook, run code, make independent analytical decisions, or "storyboard" the narrative in our dashboard.
